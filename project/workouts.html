Find Your Workout Plan

Whether you are new to exercising or have years of experience in the gym, finding the right workout routine is an important part of reaching your goals. Fitness Unity offers three training levels to help you improve strength, build muscle, and develop a consistent fitness routine.

Choose a program that matches your current experience. Focus on proper technique, consistency, and gradually increasing the difficulty of your workouts as you become stronger.

Beginner Workout Program

Push / Pull / Legs

The beginner program is designed for people starting their fitness journey. It introduces basic strength exercises through a three-day Push, Pull, and Legs routine.

Start with lighter weights and focus on learning proper form. Rest at least one day between workouts, and gradually increase your weights as the exercises become easier.

Weekly Schedule

Day Workout
MondayPush
TuesdayRest
WednesdayPull
ThursdayRest
FridayLegs
SaturdayOptional Walking

Day 1: Push (Chest, Shoulders, Triceps)

  • Bench Press - 3 sets of 8-12 reps
  • Seated Shoulder Press - 3 sets of 10 reps
  • Incline Dumbbell Press - 2 sets of 10 reps
  • Lateral Raises - 2 sets of 12-15 reps
  • Triceps Pushdowns - 2 sets of 12 reps

Day 2: Pull (Back, Biceps)

  • Lat Pulldowns - 3 sets of 10 reps
  • Seated Cable Rows - 3 sets of 10 reps
  • Face Pulls - 2 sets of 12-15 reps
  • Dumbbell Biceps Curls - 3 sets of 10 reps
  • Hammer Curls - 2 sets of 12 reps

Day 3: Legs (Quads, Hamstrings, Glutes)

  • Goblet Squats - 3 sets of 10 reps
  • Leg Press - 3 sets of 10 reps
  • Leg Curls - 3 sets of 12 reps
  • Calf Raises - 3 sets of 15 reps
  • Plank - 3 sets of 20-30 seconds

Beginner Tip: Rest 60-120 seconds between sets. Begin with manageable weights and prioritize correct exercise form over lifting heavier.

Intermediate Workout Program

Alternating Two-Week Training Split

This program is designed for people who already understand basic gym exercises and want to improve their strength and muscle development.

The program alternates between Push, Pull, Legs during Week A and Upper Body, Lower Body during Week B. Continue alternating these weeks throughout your training journey.

Advanced Workout Programs

Advanced training is designed for experienced individuals who have developed consistent exercise habits and want to focus on specific fitness goals.

Choose the training approach that best matches your goals, whether that involves increasing muscle size, losing body fat, developing strength, improving conditioning, or bodybuilding.

Muscle Building / Bulking

Focus on progressive overload, sufficient recovery, and a moderate calorie surplus to support muscle growth.

Example 5-Day Schedule

  • Monday: Push - Chest, Shoulders, Triceps
  • Tuesday: Pull - Back, Biceps
  • Wednesday: Legs - Quads, Hamstrings, Glutes
  • Thursday: Rest
  • Friday: Upper Body - Compound Exercises
  • Saturday: Lower Body - Legs and Core
  • Sunday: Rest

Main exercises include bench presses, squats, rows, pull-ups, and Romanian deadlifts. Perform 3-4 sets of 6-12 reps for most exercises, adjusting volume and recovery based on performance.

Cutting / Fat Loss

The goal of cutting is to reduce body fat while maintaining as much muscle and strength as possible.

Example Weekly Schedule

  • Monday: Upper Body Strength
  • Tuesday: Lower Body Strength
  • Wednesday: Moderate Cardio
  • Thursday: Upper Body Hypertrophy
  • Friday: Lower Body Hypertrophy
  • Saturday: Walking or Cycling
  • Sunday: Rest

Continue resistance training with 3-4 sets of 6-12 reps on major exercises. Combine training with a manageable calorie deficit, adequate protein, and sustainable cardio.

Strength Training

Strength-focused training emphasizes increasing performance in major compound exercises such as squats, bench presses, and deadlifts.

Example 4-Day Schedule

  • Monday: Heavy Squats - 4 sets of 3-5 reps
  • Tuesday: Heavy Bench Press - 4 sets of 3-5 reps
  • Wednesday: Rest
  • Thursday: Deadlifts - 3 sets of 3-5 reps
  • Friday: Overhead Press - 4 sets of 5 reps
  • Saturday: Light Recovery Activity
  • Sunday: Rest

Complete each lifting day with 2-3 accessory exercises for supporting muscle groups. Rest 2-4 minutes between heavy sets and avoid sacrificing technique for weight.

Bodybuilding / Hypertrophy

Bodybuilding training focuses on developing muscle size, proportions, and balanced muscular development.

Example 5-Day Schedule

  • Monday: Chest and Triceps
  • Tuesday: Back and Biceps
  • Wednesday: Legs and Calves
  • Thursday: Rest
  • Friday: Shoulders and Arms
  • Saturday: Full Body Weak-Point Training
  • Sunday: Rest

Use a combination of compound movements and isolation exercises. Most exercises can include 3-4 sets of 8-15 repetitions, with attention to controlled movement and adequate recovery.

Cardiovascular / Endurance Training

This program focuses on improving cardiovascular endurance, stamina, and overall conditioning. It can include running, cycling, rowing, and interval training.

Example Weekly Schedule

  • Monday: Easy Run - 30 minutes
  • Tuesday: Full Body Strength Training
  • Wednesday: Cycling - 40 minutes
  • Thursday: Interval Training - 20 minutes
  • Friday: Rest
  • Saturday: Longer Easy Run - 45-60 minutes
  • Sunday: Walking and Mobility

Adjust intensity according to your fitness level. Increase endurance gradually and include easier sessions between more demanding workouts.

Advanced Tip: These are example programs, not requirements to train every day. Adjust weekly volume, exercise selection, and recovery based on your experience and individual needs.

Workout Tips for Every Fitness Level

  • Warm up before every training session.
  • Focus on proper exercise technique.
  • Increase training difficulty gradually.
  • Stay hydrated throughout your workouts.
  • Include rest days for muscle recovery.
  • Track your progress and stay consistent.
  • Stop if you experience sharp or unusual pain.

Everyone progresses at a different pace. Choose a plan that works for your schedule, abilities, and goals. Consistency and recovery are just as important as training.
